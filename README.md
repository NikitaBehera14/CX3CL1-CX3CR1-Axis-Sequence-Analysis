Evolutionary Conservation of the CX3CL1–CX3CR1 Axis

1. Project Overview

The CX3CL1–CX3CR1 axis is a ligand–receptor signalling system consisting of CX3CL1 (fractalkine), a chemokine, and CX3CR1, its cognate G protein-coupled receptor. This axis is involved in immune-cell recruitment, adhesion, and signalling. Emerging interest in the relationship between immune signalling and metabolic regulation motivates further investigation of its potential metabolic relevance.

This ongoing research project investigates the evolutionary conservation of CX3CL1 and CX3CR1 across different organisms using comparative sequence analysis. By examining sequence similarity, conservation, and divergence, the project aims to explore whether evolutionary patterns may provide insights into the functional constraints of this signalling axis and its potential relevance to metabolic regulation.

2. Research Question

How conserved are CX3CL1 and CX3CR1 across different organisms, and can comparative sequence analysis provide insights into their potential roles in metabolic regulation?

3. Research Objectives

- Retrieve and organise CX3CL1 and CX3CR1 sequences from selected organisms.
- Compare sequences to investigate evolutionary conservation and divergence.
- Perform computational multiple sequence alignment to identify conserved and variable regions.
- Examine patterns of sequence similarity across organisms.
- Interpret the observed patterns in the context of the known biology of the CX3CL1–CX3CR1 axis and its potential metabolic relevance.

4. Computational Approach

The computational component of this project focuses on developing a reproducible Python-based workflow for sequence analysis.

The planned workflow includes:

1. Organising protein sequences in FASTA format.
2. Reading and validating sequence records using Biopython.
3. Performing multiple sequence alignment using an appropriate alignment tool.
4. Analysing alignment outputs to examine conserved and variable positions.
5. Generating summary tables and visualisations to support comparative interpretation.

The scripts and notebooks will document the analysis steps, parameters, and outputs as the workflow develops.

5. Current Progress

- Sequence retrieval across selected organisms has been completed.
- FASTA-formatted sequence data have been collected for CX3CL1 and CX3CR1.
- Initial sequence alignment has been performed manually.
- Python-based analysis and reproducible alignment workflows are being developed.

7. Biological Interpretation and Limitations

Sequence conservation may indicate evolutionary constraints on protein structure or function. However, conservation alone cannot establish a direct metabolic role or demonstrate that the CX3CL1–CX3CR1 axis acts as a metabolic modulator.

The computational findings will therefore be interpreted alongside relevant biological literature, with further analyses considered where appropriate.
